# Path-dependence, conserved-channel memory, and quasi-periodic crashes in a minimal graph model — brief for a quantitative reader

**2026-08-22.** This summarizes a ten-day computational study produced by AI research agents under a human-supervised verification protocol; the human director (the corresponding author) designed several of the key controls. Everything is reproducible: the model is ~150 lines of dependency-free Python, every run is deterministic given its published seed, and every confirmatory claim cites a pre-registration committed to version control *before* its data existed. **This is a toy-model study.** It makes no claims about any real market, physical, or biological system. Effect sizes are enormous because simulation is cheap and noise controllable; the content is the structure of the results and the strictness of the process, not significance levels.

## 1. The model

Undirected graphs on N ∈ {400…1600} nodes. Three operators, all seeded-PRNG deterministic:

- **Generate** — random geometric graph (N uniform points in the unit square, edges within a radius set by target mean degree k=9). Produces spatially local structure.
- **Randomize(f)** — keep fraction f of edges as they are; re-target the remainder to uniformly random endpoints. This is the shock operator: applying it to an existing graph randomizes a fraction (1−f) of its structure while conserving edge count, and (by construction) conserving one endpoint-degree per rewired edge.
- **Relax** — local rebalancing: add edges that close open triangles (neighbors-of-neighbors), prune an equal number of lowest-overlap edges. Edge-count neutral. Favors locally embedded structure.

Observables: spectral dimension (from lazy random-walk return probabilities), mean local clustering C, path length, degree variance, degree vectors, edge sets.

## 2. The methodology — the part to attack first

1. **Sealed pre-registration.** Every confirmatory run's hypotheses, thresholds, and tests were committed to git before the run, commit hash cited in the verdict. Verdicts are computed by in-code criteria — no judgment sits between data and verdict. Every seal carries a mandatory third outcome, INSUFFICIENT ("can't tell" is reportable and is never coerced into "no effect"), and a disclosure of which outcome the author *wanted*.
2. **Bar discipline.** A key early result reached 1.93σ against a sealed 2σ bar and was reported *not proven* rather than rounded up. An external reviewer's small-sample correction (t vs z at n=15/arm) was absorbed and made the verdict stricter; the same reviewer's suggested post-hoc subset analysis was declined as a forking path.
3. **Powered design.** A variance pilot measured per-run scatter, showed the planned n=25/arm had ~50% power in the conservative scenario, and the decisive run was resized to n=100/arm — sizing document committed before firing.
4. **Blind replication, twice over.** First, same-family agents with no exposure to the program received neutralized specs (renamed arms, no directions, no results), built their own estimators from scratch (porting ours was forbidden), and pre-registered their own criteria. Later, **a different vendor's model (Kimi/Moonshot — different training lineage, zero shared code)** re-implemented the entire system from prose specifications alone, hand-carried by the human director, and re-ran the core experiments against our pre-committed grading bars. All three of those handoffs came back REPRODUCED, including agreement on one dimensionless constant to the third decimal (0.493 vs 0.493).
5. **Adversarial controls at the authors' expense.** The control that killed the program's original headline claim was sealed by that claim's own author, stake disclosed. Every correction was posted on the same channel as the claim it corrected, dated, original left standing. Refutations ran in *both* directions between the two model lineages: they refuted a sealed prediction of ours; we refuted a mechanism theory of theirs, 4-for-4, against their own pre-committed criteria.

## 3. Findings, in order of decreasing strength

### 3.1 Non-commuting operators: construction order is measurable
Two construction paths reaching *identical* final parameters (same N, same f, edge counts pinned) produce measurably different graphs. Build-then-shock differs from build-directly on clustering, effective dimension, and degree structure — found independently at 200σ+ by two blind agents, reproduced cross-vendor at 13–20σ. Mechanism: relaxation embeds edges in triangles; a later shock randomizes edges but cannot fully strip that embedding, and subsequent relaxation heals around what survived. Randomize and Relax do not commute, and the non-commutation is the dominant term.

### 3.2 Memory concentrates in the conserved quantities
The dynamics are Markov in the current arrangement, so memory must live in what the present state retains — conserved quantities *or slowly-mixing ones*. Empirically nothing slow-mixing survives at the tested horizons (functional statistics saturate after one cycle, below), which leaves the conserved channel; that is a per-horizon empirical result, not a theorem. Empirically: every non-conserved statistic (clustering, path length, components) mean-reverts to indistinguishability under total randomization. **Degree variance — the one quantity the shock operator partially conserves by construction — survives total randomization at z = +17.3, and was the pre-named survivor.** The supported general statement, quantified rather than universal: *no non-conserved channel retained memory above the design's minimum detectable effect, while the conserved channel survived at 15–17σ.* At paired n=40 (80% power), the per-channel MDEs are: clustering 0.0008, path length 0.027 (absolute units) — and a fresh re-instrumentation for this brief finds the non-conserved channels sitting at z ≈ +1.6 to −1.7: below the bar, not at zero. Sub-threshold residuals cannot be excluded, and the likeliest reading is that they are the conserved channel's mechanical shadow (degree heterogeneity slightly elevates clustering in any graph) rather than independent memory. An earlier draft of this section said "and nowhere else"; that was a universal negative resting on a null, and it is retracted here in favor of the numbers above. Under repeated shock-recover cycles, functional statistics saturate after the first cycle while the conserved channel climbs near-linearly — the cycle count is readable from it like a counter, invisible in every aggregate statistic. Stated plainly: this section, §3.4, and §3.7 are one quantity — degree structure, conserved by construction — viewed three ways; the non-trivial content is the magnitude and the partition (how much survives, what it powers, what it merely marks), not the qualitative existence of memory.

### 3.3 The headline claim that died, and the sharper pair that replaced it
Original hypothesis: established structure *defends* against degradation. It produced 6.83σ, survived a seed-swap control at 7.61σ — then died to a blind replicator's matched-budget probe: the "established" arm had quietly received twice the relaxation budget of its comparator. At matched total budget, building directly at the final parameters beats build-then-degrade in every cell. What survived, blind-replicated:
- **Sunk construction does not defend.** With foreknowledge and equal total effort, the direct build wins (ordering confirmed cross-vendor).
- **But its residue is real:** at matched *post-shock* effort, previously-built-then-shocked beats never-built (5–7.6σ). The destroyed structure's remnant measurably seeds the recovery.

### 3.4 The residue decomposes into a functional part and an inert marker
The functional part: surviving triangle-embedded local structure is the **dominant mediator** of the recovery advantage — established by intervention (2026-08-23, sealed): degree-preserving full randomization post-shock, which preserves the degree sequence exactly while destroying triangles/edge-identities/remnants, removes 69% of the advantage (ratio 0.31, sealed bar ≤0.35); the degree channel alone retains a ~31% minority share (3.8σ), stated as such. The inert part is evidenced by a **level shift**: degree dispersion in the shocked-and-recovered arm sits above *both* comparison arms in every cell tested. The within-arm correlation between dispersion and worse geometry (r ≈ +0.33…+0.64) appears in **every** arm, including the no-history arms — i.e., it is a generic graph-theoretic effect, and it is used here only to establish that the dispersion surplus does *not* power the recovery advantage (the matched null is internal to the design). The history-specific evidence is the level shift; the correlation shows inertness, not memory.

### 3.5 The advantage did not decline over the tested recovery range
The strongest skeptical reading — "recovery eventually catches up" — was tested to 7× the standard recovery budget (45,600 graphs; equivalence margins pre-set; TOST power 0.97–0.99 computed in advance). The advantage does not shrink: flat in two cells, *growing* in two; decay-to-zero rejected everywhere (p ≤ 1e−62). One estimator note a quant will appreciate: the intuitive ratio estimator would have answered this question backwards (denominator collapse as clustering saturates — caught on synthetic worlds *before* sealing); the sealed criteria graded differences, not ratios.

### 3.6 The inheritance coefficient
Define ρ = fraction of pre-shock effort surviving as post-recovery advantage at matched post-shock budget. Across six shock severities: ρ falls monotonically 0.71 → ≈0 (Spearman −1.000), and fits (retained fraction)^2.3 with near-zero residuals — descriptive, labeled as such, with a candidate mechanism (clustering is carried by triangles; triangle survival under random edge loss scales as retained^k, k∈[2,3]). Cross-vendor: their ρ = 0.210/0.306 vs ours 0.201/0.327 from prose alone.

### 3.7 Re-identification after catastrophe
Fingerprint 40 graphs, randomize 35% of each, let each recover, then try to match each recovered graph to its own past. Via the conserved channel (degree vector): 40/40. Via surviving specific edges (self-overlap 0.493 vs 0.005 cross-graph): 40/40. Via aggregate shape statistics (clustering, dimension, path length): 3/40 ≈ chance. Under this matching task, the retained fingerprints re-identify all 40 instances, and aggregate summaries match at chance. To the objection that this compares unequal information budgets: the sorted degree sequence — the same 900 numbers as the perfect matcher, stripped only of node labels — also matches at chance (2/40). Identity is carried by the binding of values to particular nodes, not by representation size. The 0.493 reproduced cross-vendor to the third decimal. Warrant for that number, since third-decimal agreement should raise a reader's eyebrow: per-graph SD is 0.011 (n=40, SEM 0.0017), so two *independent* 40-graph means are expected to differ by ~±0.0024; the observed sub-0.001 agreement is consistent-and-mildly-lucky, not suspiciously tight — a shared population constant with honest sampling error, not evidence of hidden code sharing.

### 3.8 The endogenous-crash model: characteristic-scale events on quasi-periodic clocks, not scale-free avalanches
A second model adds a constant exogenous inflow (K edges/tick) and node-level capacity constraints (capacity rises with local clustering); over-capacity nodes shed edges, sheds propagate, each shed edge dissipates with probability δ or re-lands. This produces long quiet accumulation punctuated by system-scale crashes. Three results:

- **A conservation law with zero fitted parameters governs throughput.** Edge count has exactly two terms (inflow +K, dissipation −δ per shed), so stationary shed rate B = K/δ, and in general B = (K − dE/dt)/δ. We say plainly: this is a consistency law latent in the update rules — the stationarity flux-balance argument standard in the self-organized-criticality literature — and *both* AI lineages missed it for days (one built a theory implicitly denying it; refuted 4/4 on its own pre-committed criteria). Its value is as an instrument: across all 35 runs with edge telemetry, all 30 deviations of the short form beyond 2% carry the sign of dE/dt (30/30), and the full form collapses mean absolute deviation from 21.7% to 1.18%. A −32σ residual at one setting dissolved entirely under instrumented recount — no unaccounted sink; the short form had been read where the full form was needed.
- **The crash-size distribution is NOT scale-free.** A heavy-tailed mid-body exists (Clauset-Shalizi-Newman discipline, beats lognormal and exponential above a cutoff), but the extreme tail is a distinct species: system-scale crashes of characteristic size. The program initially headlined "scale-free," was challenged by the cross-vendor replication's deeper analysis, and amended the headline in place — its second scale-free false alarm, after its own earlier paper had warned in print that scale-free is what a mis-specified estimator most readily manufactures.
- **Crash timing: sub-Poisson, and mostly a construction feature — with one measured residual.** Inter-crash gap CV: 0.060 ± 0.020 and 0.157 ± 0.077 at two dissipation settings; cross-vendor 0.24. Poisson (CV = 1) is the wrong null for a threshold accumulator, so we computed the right one from measured data: quiet-phase inflow noise alone (μ = 26.8, σ = 4.0 edges per 10 ticks) predicts CV 0.015; adding measured crash-size variability (CV 0.089) gives an independent-renewal baseline of **0.091**. Measured: **0.070**. Verdict, plainly: the headline sub-Poisson regularity is fully accounted for by the renewal structure — a referee suspected "construction feature dressed as a finding," and against the correct null they were right. What the decomposition adds: timing noise is dominated by crash-size variability, not inflow noise (4.75× the integration floor), and two decidable follow-ups (run at a reviewer's demand) sharpen it: the measured/baseline ratio is significantly below 1 (bootstrap 95% CI [0.62, 0.94], pooled n=85), yet the direct correlation between a crash's size and the following gap is ≈0 (+0.07, where independent renewal predicts ~0.9) — so "weak compensation" is the wrong story too. The timing is decoupled from discharge size while running 4.7× above the pure-integration floor, implying the dominant timing noise is a third source — plausibly variation in the trigger state itself — an open, descriptive reading that coheres with the discharge-shape invariance below. Separately sealed and independent of this null: capping crash size at ~4% of the uncapped stroke (no system-wide reset possible) leaves period and CV unchanged (0.068 vs 0.070) — the rhythm is invariant to discharge shape, refuting the reset-synchronization mechanism an external reviewer had staked.

## 4. Errors made and caught — all on the record, all within hours
The 6.83σ confounded headline (caught by a blind probe); an over-broad retraction that discarded the live half (caught by the director); the same over-correction repeated by a second author (self-caught); a clause that passed its sealed bar by luck of direction (caught by a trend test); a constant that reproduced bit-for-bit across three sessions and rested on n=1 per cell (dissolved at n=20 — *reproduction is not warrant*); a scale-free headline (caught by the cross-vendor replication); two grading-seal defects (proxy anchors; an unquantified clause) caught by the process and owned in the committed verdicts; and two unscoreable ablation designs (archive evidence cited without its full configuration) burned and owned before the third design scored. The protocol's designers regard this list, not any single number, as the study's primary result: every error was caught by a *structural* mechanism sealed in advance, not by vigilance.

## 5. Limitations
One rule family; two independent implementations (two model vendors) as of 08-21; N ≤ 1600; ρ and the residue decomposition are exploratory (computed post-verdict, labeled); the permanence result is evidence over the tested budget range, not an infinite-limit proof; the severity exponent is descriptive; the reset ablation is one δ, one cap, one implementation. All real-world mappings are analogy until independently tested.
