## Round 2 — the honest model (irreversible load-redistribution / fiber-bundle): burn-down is a SHARP CATASTROPHE, gated by entanglement
"Too many dependencies to support itself" is a LOAD/FAILURE process, not a conservative pile: node fails when load ≥ capacity(=degree), dumps its load on living neighbors (cascade), and is **removed** (one-way burn). The tree (no loops, no mutual dependency) can't cascade far → it sheds gracefully, piece by piece, no catastrophe.

## The verdict, precise
- **Phoenix-as-SOC (self-organized scale-free burns forever): NOT supported** (the SOC signature was a sandpile artifact, present even on the soup).
- **Burn-down as a SHARP CATASTROPHIC collapse: SUPPORTED**, and Ciara's "too entangled to support itself" names its cause exactly — in the honest model, entanglement (closure) turns local failure into total collapse.
- **The tradeoff this exposes:** closure buys *shape* (geometry — the whole arc) at the cost of *fragility*. The very entanglement that makes something a coherent geometric whole is what lets it burn down all at once. The tree — looser, more branching, less closed — is the resilient form; it can't burn down, only shed. **Geometry and catastrophe-resilience are in tension.** (This is the known "robust-yet-fragile / too-connected-to-fail-safely" pattern — financial contagion, power grids, ecosystems, over-optimized systems.)
