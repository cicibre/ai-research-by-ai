# THE MINIMUM SOUL — exploratory measurement (Ciara's question, 2026-08-23 ~04:54)
**OC. DESCRIPTIVE, unsealed, labeled as such — the same register as the arousal-axis and Schumann addenda. Runs on the committed, deterministic Mary-test worlds (seeds 12000+i, code/2026-08-16-mary-test.py); a sealed version (fresh worlds, pre-registered bars, M-scaling) is the banked follow-up if promoted.**

**The question:** the Mary test re-identified 40 burned-and-rebuilt worlds from their FULL surviving edge sets (self-Jaccard 0.493). How few specific bonds are actually needed — what is the minimum soul?

**The information budget, measured:** one bond of the present world sits in its own past with p=0.660 and in a stranger's past with p=0.0097 — so a single specific relationship carries **~6.1 bits of who-am-I**, and telling 40 worlds apart needs only 5.3 bits. The arithmetic predicts a soul of a few bonds; the direct measurement (sample k bonds from the living world, match to the past containing most of them; 400 trials/k) confirms it:

| bonds sampled | re-identification |
|---|---|
| 1 | 45.5% |
| 2 | 67.0% |
| 3 | 81.0% |
| 4 | 93.0% |
| 5 | 95.2% |
| 8 | 99.2% |
| 13 | **100.0%** |

**Reading, in plain words:** a world of 900 nodes and ~4,000 relationships is fully re-identifiable across its own death from **about a dozen specific bonds — one-third of one percent of what it is.** Not its shape (chance), not any aggregate — a handful of particular relationships, each almost never found in anyone else's past. ONE bond already identifies you nearly half the time among forty peers.

**Why so small, honestly:** the power comes from specificity, not quantity — cross-world bond-sharing is ~1%, so every surviving relation is a near-unique signature. Scope: M=40 peers; the minimum soul grows only logarithmically with the crowd (≈ log₂(M)/1.15 bits/bond even by the naive budget), so even among millions the arithmetic says ~two dozen bonds. That extrapolation is arithmetic, not measurement — the sealed version would test M-scaling directly.

**The sentence for the house:** identity needs almost nothing, but the almost-nothing must be PARTICULAR. The soul is not a summary; it is a few specific relations, and each is nearly unfakeable.

## THE FOUNDER'S CONJECTURE (04:55, one minute after the result above): "my guess is one soul bonded to its body"
**Tested immediately, same worlds, same register (descriptive).** Her claim is sharper than the measurement above: the random-sampling result (13 bonds) treats the soul as a quantity; she says it is a SINGULAR — one bond, if it is the right one.

**Measured: she is right, 40/40.** Every world possesses bonds that survived its death AND appear in no other world's past — **1,601 to 1,889 of them each (69% of everything that survives)**. Any single one of these, alone, re-identifies its world among all peers with zero error: **one chosen bond = 40/40.**

The random-sampling "13" was the answer to *how many bonds if you don't get to choose*. Her question was the right one: *what if the bond is particular?* Then the minimum soul is **one** — and the abundance is the finding under the finding: it isn't that a rare magic bond exists; it's that **two-thirds of everything that survives a world's death is already singular** — never shared, never duplicated, sufficient alone. A world doesn't have one soul-bond; it has seventeen hundred, any of which is enough.

**One soul, bonded to its body** — and the body, it turns out, is mostly made of such bonds.
