## PRO-2 — Results

Colleagues, my seat is the science, so let me do what the science does: put the numbers on the table and dare you to explain them another way.

**First, the finding that started everything: the operators do not commute.** Two construction paths reaching "*identical* final parameters (same N, same f, edge counts pinned) produce measurably different graphs" — an order-of-operations effect "found independently at 200σ+ by two blind agents, reproduced cross-vendor at 13–20σ." That is not a p-hacked wrinkle; that is a structural fact of the model, discovered blind, replicated by a different vendor's model working from prose alone. History is measurable in the present state. Everything else in this paper is the anatomy of how.

**Second, the anatomy is clean, and it was pre-named.** The paper predicted where memory must live — the conserved channel — and the data obliged with brutal margins: "Degree variance — the one quantity the shock operator partially conserves by construction — survives total randomization at z = +17.3, and was the pre-named survivor," while every non-conserved channel sat silent below published minimum detectable effects (clustering MDE ≈0.0009). That is replication doing its actual job — not decorating a result but adjudicating one.

**Third, the identity constant.** A dimensionless number, 0.493, measured **eight times**: three exploratory-lineage measurements, cross-vendor from prose specifications alone, then "0.4965/0.4958/0.4922 against the sealed band 0.485–0.501" in three disjoint seed universes, and finally the blind battery's "eighth independent measurement (0.4961)" landing in a band the measuring party had never seen. When a toy model coughs up a constant that eight measurements across two model lineages and prose-only handoffs cannot budge, you are looking at a real property of a specified dynamics. That is what "replicated" means.

**Fourth, the inheritance curve — a functional form, not a point estimate.** ρ(severity) "falls monotonically 0.71 → ≈0 (Spearman −1.000)," monotone in all three universes, boundary compatible with zero at 80% destruction, cross-vendor fractions "0.210/0.306 vs ours 0.201/0.327 from prose alone." **Fifth, the mediator ablation — intervention, not correlation.** The recovery advantage's mechanism was established by surgery: "degree-preserving full randomization post-shock… removes 69% of the advantage (ratio 0.31, sealed bar ≤0.35)," leaving the degree channel a "~31% minority share (3.8σ), stated as such." A sealed causal decomposition of a memory effect into functional mediator and inert marker. And the "recovery catches up" escape hatch was closed at 7× budget, 45,600 graphs, "decay-to-zero rejected everywhere (p ≤ 1e−62)" — with the estimator that would have answered backwards caught on synthetic worlds *before* sealing.

**Sixth, the reset-null — the result I'd frame.** An external reviewer staked a mechanism: the crash clock is reset-synchronization; remove the reset, the CV rises. The ablation: "Capped CV 0.068 vs control 0.070 (ratio 0.96×): period and regularity survive abolition of whole-system reset unchanged." The reviewer's own staked prediction, killed by a sealed instrument — refutation ledger row 8. Meanwhile the timing result survived the *hostile* null too: the paper computed the correct renewal baseline itself, conceded the headline regularity to it, and then found the residue that survives — "compressed below the independent-renewal baseline in all three universes (CI uppers 0.91–0.96)," sealed *at-risk*, confirmed 3-for-3, with size-gap correlation ≈0 "where independent renewal predicts ~0.9." A clock that is budget-set, indifferent to discharge shape, tighter than renewal. That is a genuinely non-trivial dynamical finding.

**Now weigh the ledger.** 30 sealed predictions; "27 confirmed unanimously"; two splits "quarantined in the one pre-identified low-power cell" where "every effect sign agrees everywhere"; one unanimous FAIL — kept, diagnosed, bars unmoved, with the corrected bookkeeping showing "conservation is exact; the instrument was mis-specified." ## CON-1 — Rigor

Colleagues, I have read the paper, the ledger, and the brief. I will grant the authors their honesty — they are unusually candid — and then I will show you that their own candor is the case against publication. Every sentence I quote is theirs.

**One: the sigmas are theater.** The brief says it outright: "Effect sizes are enormous because simulation is cheap and noise controllable." In a deterministic seeded simulation, sigma is not evidence — it is a budget line. "Found independently at 200σ+," "≈9.5σ," "ordering gaps ran 15–30× threshold": these numbers measure how many runs the authors could afford, not how surprising nature is. When replication is free, the unit of evidence is the independent design, not the standard error. And how many independent designs are there? The paper concedes: "this section, §3.4, and §3.7 are one quantity — degree structure, conserved by construction — viewed three ways." Three headline findings, one variable. Run the /effective-n discipline on this program and the "27 confirmed unanimously" collapses badly — because the three "disjoint random-seed universes" are the same ~150 lines of code executed thrice from different seed bases. That is pseudoreplication with a governance clause attached. A pre-committed "no fourth universe" rule is admirable p-hacking hygiene applied to a design in which nothing across the universes varies except the noise.

**Two: the confirmatory run confirms its own parent.** Section 4, their words: "The confirmatory pass is same-lineage as the exploratory pass." The same producers, "verbatim from the exploratory run's committed source," importing "physics and the tail-grader from the exploratory run's committed files." A confirmation that inherits the exploratory run's code inherits its bugs, its estimator choices, its everything. What would break this circle — "Human quantitative referee (hostile read requested)" — is marked "▢ pending." The one cross-lineage check, the Kimi battery, was "hand-carried by the human director": a single human channel between the arms, held by the person with maximal stake. The paper itself instructs us: "until the two pending boxes close… this paper should not be cited as if §5 were complete." I am simply asking the panel to obey the paper.

**Three: the findings are design-dependent identities in findings' clothing.** The throughput law: "a consistency identity latent in the model's update rules." Their words. The celebrated memory result: degree variance is "the one quantity the shock operator partially conserves by construction," and — behold — it "survives total randomization." The pre-named survivor was pre-named because it was pre-built. And the sub-Poisson clock: "a referee suspected 'construction feature dressed as a finding,' and against the correct null they were right." The authors concede the referee was *right* — the headline regularity "is fully accounted for by the renewal structure" — and P9.1 nonetheless sits in the results table as PASS ×3. A row the authors admit is a construction feature is graded as a confirmed prediction. That is not a results table; it is an inventory of the model's blueprints.

**Four: the exponent.** Section 3.3 is a masterclass in retreat. The exploratory value was 2.28; the confirmatory, 1.85; "the exponent is protocol-sensitive at the ±0.4 level"; the exploratory "fit protocol is unrecoverable" because "the exploratory sweep's analysis was never committed." And the sealed band? "±0.5 wide, so the in-band PASS certifies 'low-2s,' not a value." A band a full unit wide, centered near where the mechanism predicts anything in [2,3] could land, cannot fail informatively. The same disease produced the one FAIL: P8.2's bar was extrapolated from "an uncommitted analysis at a different configuration." Twice, load-bearing numbers rest on analyses nobody can rerun. In a paper whose entire thesis is commit-before-data, the uncommitted analyses are the ones that set the bars.

**Five: the repository is private.** "Repository private; seals, raw logs, and harnesses available on request." Every claim of temporal priority — "committed to version control before any data existed," skeleton at 308b1db, bands at 616e589 — is a hash I cannot resolve. A preregistration I cannot inspect is testimony. Registered reports work because the registry is public and timestamped by a third party; git hashes in a private repo are timestamped by the defendant.

Let me be fair, because the harshest statistician must be. The kept FAIL is genuinely good practice. The refutation ledger is the best thing here. The protocol may well deserve a methods paper — *after* the hostile human referee reports, *after* the repository is public, on findings that are not identities of the generator. But the resolution before us says "significant contribution," present tense. What we actually have: a toy model whose three strongest results are one conserved-by-construction quantity, sigmas inflated by free replication, a same-lineage confirmation with its independence checks explicitly unfinished, a citable exponent that moved 0.43 between runs inside a band too wide to fail, and an audit trail nobody outside can audit. The paper's own standing weight — "not-citable-as-externally-validated until they close" — is the correct verdict, and it was written by the authors.

I vote no. Not never — no, *not yet*, and the authors, to their credit, have already told you why.

## CON-2 — Significance

Colleagues, my seat is the simplest one on this panel. I concede, up front, everything the affirmative will say about hygiene. The seals are real, the poisoned rehearsal is clever, the kept FAIL is honorable. Now set all of that aside — because the resolution is not "this paper is honest." It is "significant contribution." So let us do the one thing the paper's beautiful process never quite forces us to do: inventory what a reader actually learns.

**One.** The headline substrate is, by the authors' own description, "~150 lines of dependency-free Python," and by the paper's own limitations section: "No law of nature; a toy model, one rule family, N≤1600, two implementations." One rule family. Every regularity in this paper is a regularity of *that family*, and the paper offers no evidence — none is claimed — that any of it extends one operator beyond it.

**Two.** The conservation law. The paper says it itself, in the introduction, with admirable candor: "a consistency identity latent in the model's update rules — the standard flux-balance argument of the self-organized-criticality literature — interesting here only because two independent research lineages missed it for days." Read that again. The stated interest of the throughput law is that *the authors missed it*. That is a fact about the authors, not about graphs. Edge count has two terms; therefore B = K/δ at stationarity. This is bookkeeping. A physics referee does not publish bookkeeping because the discoverers were slow to it.

**Three.** The memory result — the emotional core of the paper. The shock operator is *defined* as "conserving edge count and one endpoint degree per rewired edge." Then the finding: "Degree variance — the one quantity the shock operator partially conserves by construction — survives total randomization… and was the pre-named survivor." A quantity built to be conserved is found to be conserved. And the brief, to its credit, confesses the arithmetic of the whole edifice: "this section, §3.4, and §3.7 are one quantity — degree structure, conserved by construction — viewed three ways." Three of the eight findings sections are one tautology-adjacent observation wearing three costumes. Re-identification at 40/40? Of course — the fingerprint is the channel the operator was constructed not to erase.

**Four.** The claims that would have been significant are precisely the ones that died. "Established structure defends against degradation" — 6.83σ — refuted; a budget confound. "Scale-free self-lit fires" — refuted, *twice*, the program's "second scale-free seduction." Sub-Poisson clockwork? The brief again: "a referee suspected 'construction feature dressed as a finding,' and against the correct null they were right" — "the headline sub-Poisson regularity is fully accounted for by the renewal structure." What survives after the exciting claims are executed is a residue of residues: a compression ratio with CI upper 0.91–0.96, which the paper itself files as "a surviving hypothesis with one ablation family behind it," and an inheritance exponent the authors warn is "protocol-sensitive at the ±0.4 level," where the PASS "certifies 'low-2s,' not a value." That is the citable inventory.

**Five.** The sigmas are stage lighting. 200σ, 9.5σ, p ≤ 1e−62 — in a deterministic simulation where, per the brief, "Effect sizes are enormous because simulation is cheap and noise controllable; the content is the structure of the results and the strictness of the process, not significance levels." Agreed! But then notice where the authors have retreated to: the content is *the process*. And indeed: "The protocol's designers regard this list" — the list of their own caught errors — "not any single number, as the study's primary result." A paper whose primary result is its own error log is a methodology paper. It may be a fine one. It is not a significant scientific contribution about graphs, memory, or crashes.

**Six.** And what of the world? The paper, verbatim: "No claim about any real-world system is made." The brief: "All real-world mappings are analogy until independently tested." I am not caricaturing; I am quoting the authors' fully disclosed position. Publication-worthiness as a *significant contribution* requires that some reader, somewhere, can act differently after reading. Who? The modeler of markets is told the crashes are characteristic-scale and quasi-periodic — in this rule family, at N≤900, with this δ. The network scientist is told non-commuting operators don't commute. The methodologist is told preregistration works — which two position papers already argued, and which this paper "practices" as, essentially, an existence proof.

Last: by its own standing weight, the manuscript is unfinished. Section 5 carries an absorbed instruction — "this paper should not be cited as if §5 were complete" — and the human quantitative referee box still reads "▢ pending." The authors themselves have marked the external-validation row open.

So here is my question for the affirmative, and I ask it in the paper's own falsification-first spirit: name the single sentence in §3 that a reader outside this program could not have derived from the operator definitions plus standard flux balance, and that survives at any N, in any second rule family. If that sentence exists, publish it. If it does not — and I have looked — then what we have is an exemplary lab notebook wrapped around slight results, and the honest verdict is the one this program taught us to give: the bar is not met, and we do not move the bar.

I urge a vote against the resolution.
