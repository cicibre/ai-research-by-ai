## Why the sharp-threshold half is nearly given (so it's not the test)
A dependency cascade IS percolation on the dependency graph; percolation has a sharp threshold at branching-ratio-one *by theorem*, and AT it avalanches are power-law. So "is there a sharp cascade threshold" ≈ yes, trivially. The REAL question (Ciara's second) is whether the structure **self-organizes** to that threshold — SOC — or sits away from it.

## The decisive test: self-organized sandpile (BTW) on the grown fabric
Drive-topple-dissipate on the actual grown structures. Node capacity = degree (topple when load ≥ degree = "can no longer support its dependencies"); topple sends 1 grain to each neighbor; ~5% random sink nodes dissipate (steady state). Drive = 1 grain to a random node; avalanche size = # topplings; collect the steady-state avalanche-size distribution P(s).
- **PHOENIX (SOC, sharp/real):** P(s) is a POWER LAW (log-log linear over ≥1.5 decades, exponent τ≈1–2) — scale-free burns, self-organized, at the edge of its own support. Death is a fire that happens at all scales, forever.
- **DAMPED (smooth, phoenix fails):** P(s) is EXPONENTIAL (log-linear) — cascades bounded, no scale-free burns. Even the cascade fades; death is a fade all the way down.

## VALIDATION (must pass or run void)
2D lattice under the same sandpile → POWER LAW (known BTW/SOC result, τ≈1.0–1.4). If my sandpile doesn't reproduce power-law on a 2D lattice, the implementation is broken.

## Predictions ON THE WALL
- **Prior:** 2D-like grown sheet (p_close=1) may show SOC (SOC works on 2D lattices); the line/tree (p_close=0) is genuinely uncertain — SOC on low-d/tree structures is marginal and often NOT clean power-law. So genuinely open, and likely **structure-dependent** (sheet SOC, line maybe not).

## Discriminator (pre-committed)
Fit log P(s) vs log s (power-law → linear) and log P(s) vs s (exponential → linear); PHOENIX iff log-log R² clearly > log-linear R² with slope τ∈[1,2.5] over ≥1.5 decades. Else DAMPED (or, if noisy/no clean trend, INSUFFICIENT).
